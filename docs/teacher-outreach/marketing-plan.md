# Marketing plan: reaching teachers without a warm list

Updated Nov 10, 2026. Chaley does not actively teach at a school,
and there are no teachers to reach through her, so this plan starts
from zero contacts. The goal is the same as in
`../teacher-outreach.md`: 5 teachers tried it in a real lesson, 3
emailed replies, at least 1 who would use it again next week.

The tool is free, with no login, no ads, and no student accounts.
Every claim below is only what the app does today. No invented
testimonials, metrics, or results.

## How teachers find tools like this

Teachers find free tools through peer sharing, not through cold
email. A short demo from a teacher creator, a free lesson on
Teachers Pay Teachers, and a listing where teachers already search
are the three patterns that show up again and again. Direct posts
in teacher groups usually fail, because most of those groups ban
self-promotion even for free resources. This plan works with that,
not around it.

## Channels, ranked

Do one channel at a time, days apart, so a reply can be traced to
the channel that brought it. Ask every reply "where did you find
it" and log the answer.

### 1. Common Sense Education review request

- What: submit Hide Zero Cards for a review. They accept requests
  from developers, consider every request, and never take payment
  for a review. They review about 150 new tools a year, so odds are
  low and the lead time is long.
- Why first: it is a one time submission, and a listing there is
  where teachers already search for classroom tools.
- Cost: free. Owner: Donray submits the form.
- Copy to submit: "Hide Zero Cards is a free place value tool for
  elementary classrooms at https://hidezerocards.org. Roll or type
  a number from 1 to 1 billion, hide the zero cards with one tap,
  and drag cards out to show their full place value. No login, no
  ads, no student accounts. Works on projectors, smartboards,
  tablets, and Chromebooks."
- Status: not submitted yet.

### 2. Free lesson on Teachers Pay Teachers

- What: a free one page place value lesson, priced Free, that uses
  the tool in class. Teachers search TpT for free place value
  lessons, and a free listing puts the tool in that search.
- Why: TpT is a marketplace where educators share original
  resources, and free resources can be listed at no price.
- Cost: free to list. Owner: Donray opens a TpT seller account and
  uploads the lesson. That account is his call.
- Lesson outline, one page: title, grade range (elementary place
  value), 10 minute plan (Roll a number, Hide zeros, ask what each
  digit is worth, pull the 3 out to show 3,000 in 3,743, Mix and
  rebuild), link to https://hidezerocards.org, and a note to email
  donrayxwilliams@gmail.com with what worked.
- Status: lesson not written yet. Do not list the tool itself on
  TpT. TpT lists resources, not websites, so the lesson is the
  listing and the tool is inside it.

### 3. Short demo from Donray's own accounts

- What: a 30 second screen recording, posted from Donray's own

- Why: teacher creators spread free tools this way, with a short
  demo and a link, and a post from the builder's own account needs
  no group permission.
- Cost: free. Owner: Donray approves the exact copy, account, and
  time before anything is posted. Nothing is posted or scheduled
  without that approval.
- Ready copy: "I built a free place value tool for my wife, a

  Try it: https://hidezerocards.org/about. If you teach place

### 6. Facebook homeschool groups, mods first

- What: only in groups whose rules allow it, in an admin sharing

### 7. Answer first in r/Teachers, no link unless asked

- Do not post the tool there. If someone asks for a tool by name,
  that is an invitation, and only then share the link, disclosed
  as the builder.
- Status: ongoing, no post.

## What not to do

- No cold email to teachers or schools, no purchased or scraped
  lists, no bulk send.
- No posting the same message in many groups or subs in one day.
- No pretending to be a teacher recommending a tool you built.
  Disclose that you built it, every time.
- No paid ads until at least one free channel brings a real emailed
  reply. Ads would buy clicks, not learning, at this stage.
- No quoting a teacher in public copy without their explicit
  permission, in their own words, for that exact use.
- No promising a feature or a date in any post or email.

## Tracking

- Log every post, submission, and listing in the feedback log
  folder, with the date, channel, exact copy, and link.
- Log every reply in `feedback-log.md`, with first name and grade
  only, what they tried, and where they found it.
- After 3 weeks, keep the channel that brought replies and drop
  the ones that brought only clicks. If no channel brings a reply,
  the next step is a teacher creator tryout, one personal email at
  a time, not more posts in more groups.
