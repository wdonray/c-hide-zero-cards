back to the same address. Send one personal email at a time, to one

## Body

Hi [First name],

I built a free place value tool for my wife Chaley, who teaches at
home, and I am looking for a few real teachers to try it in a real
lesson and tell me what actually happens.

It is called Hide Zero Cards, at https://hidezerocards.org

It is the digital version of place value arrow cards. You roll or type
a number, and it fans out as color coded cards, one for each digit.
Tap Hide zeros and the zero cards hide in place, so 3,743 reads as
3, 7, 4, 3 while the class talks about what each digit is worth.
Pull the 3 card out and it shows 3,000, the value that was tucked
behind it all along.

A few things that might help in your classroom:

- Roll a random number, or type your own, from 1 to 1 billion, with
  ranges from up to 100 to up to a billion.
- Mix scatters the cards so students can rebuild the number in
  place value order.
- Number Forms shows the same number in word, expanded, and unit
  form, plus standard form.
- A teacher's guide is built into the app, with a 2 minute quick
  start and classroom activities.
- It works on projectors, smartboards, tablets, and Chromebooks, in
  light or dark mode.
- It is free, with no login, no ads, and no student accounts.

Would you try it for 10 minutes in a lesson this week? If you do,
just reply to this email and tell me:

1. What grade you tried it with, and on what device.
2. What students understood faster, or did more easily, with the
   cards.
3. What confused students, or slowed the lesson down.
4. What is missing for your next place value lesson.
5. Would you use it again next week? Why or why not?

That is the whole ask. I read every reply myself, and I fix what
teachers tell me is broken. If something in it does not work for
your class, I would rather hear that than a polite yes.

Thank you for teaching, and for considering it.
